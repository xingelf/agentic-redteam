What a Breach Costs the Share Price

When a public company discloses a major cyber incident, does the market punish it — and does it matter which market? This is an event study of twenty-four incidents across the US, Japan, and Europe. For each one we model the stock's normal relationship to its home index, then measure the abnormal return around the disclosure date, so a general market move is stripped out. The headline is the cumulative abnormal return (CAR).

Method: market-model event study. For each incident, AR = Rstock − (α + β·Rmarket), with α and β estimated over a pre-event window; CAR is the running sum. The average line is the cumulative average abnormal return (CAAR). Each incident is measured against its home-country index in the same currency: S&P 500 (US), Nikkei 225 (Japan), and FTSE 100 / DAX / CAC 40 / OMXC25 / OSEBX (Europe).

Prices: public market data API (adjusted close). Disclosure dates: public filings and reporting. No client, target, or engagement data. Not investment advice.
